Mark Kois and I were reminiscing about the bar at lunch yesterday. And he is the third person that I talked to about it since Lisa died and everyone has similar memories. It really was a wonderful place. He told me a story about how one thanksgiving the bar was closed and the door locked, but all the regulars had thanksgiving there. Some guy knocked on the door wanting to come have a drink and they let him in because why not. So apparently this guy gets belligerent and wants to fight someone and one of the regulars just was like "buddy, what are you doing? You want to fight someone here? We are all best friends who would do anything for each other and you are wanting to fight when we let you join us? Just get out of here." I think the thing that appealed about the place is that we didn't care who you were, just that you behaved yourself and treated everyone with respect. It made for a great mixing pot of people from every place in society. From homeless folk, students, professors, professionals, etc. It was really the only place I've ever found in my life that was so welcoming.

I think when I feel home sickness it is for that place and the cabin on Lake Michigan. I know I'll probably never be at a place like the Fugue again, but I guess I can recapture the peace I get from looking at the lake. By buying a place with a view of the lake.
