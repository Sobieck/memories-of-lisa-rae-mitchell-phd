Lisa Rae Mitchell  
 

My precious memories of my dear cousin Lisa. 

How excited I was as a child, to be given the news that Lisa was born to my Uncle Ray and Aunty Robyn.  Although I was seven at the time, I knew she was very much loved and awaited by her family.  Her middle name, Rae, carrying the family name of our shared Grandpop, Raymond Mitchell, who my Uncle Ray is also named after, as is my brother Geoffrey (Raymond) and Lisa’s grandmother Rayma.  Lisa was born on the 19th September which was the day before our Grandma Rita’s birthday on the 20th.  September also being the birthday month for Aunty Robyn on the 9th. 

I first met Lisa in person as a baby (before she was one), when Uncle Ray and Aunty Robyn came over to Sydney as sadly our Grandpop had passed suddenly aged 60.  Lisa was a beautiful baby and I was in awe of meeting her, even though it was a very sad time for the family.     

Within I think another year, Uncle Ray, Aunty Robyn and Lisa moved over to New South Wales from Perth, West Australia, as Uncle Ray began studies at Avondale.  Lisa was a beautiful girl, already observant, smart, independent and an absolute picture of health.  She loved playing with Raja and Rena, the golden retrievers and their pups.  She would recite her name and address.  She would know her books off by heart, read them out loud to us and would correct even one word that may differ from what was in the book when being read to.  She wanted to do things herself and be a part of the routines, even standing on a stool to help with the washing up.   We celebrated Lisa’s second and (I think third birthdays), while they lived near to us.    

Another great memory I have during this time is our Aunty Bev, Uncle Bent, cousins Trent, Shane and Garth visiting Sydney while on leave from Indonesia, where Uncle Bent worked for the World Health Organisation.  This was before Reanna our girl cousin joined Aunty Bev and Uncle Bent’s family as the youngest of their children.   We spent time together as cousins with my brothers Peter and Geoff, at Grandma’s house in Sydney.  We were also close to Uncle Graham and Aunty Ruth McMahon (Graham first cousin to Uncle Ray and my mum Pam), spending time with them as families in Sydney.   

When Uncle Ray, Aunty Robyn and Lisa were to move to the United States for Uncle Ray to continue his studies, I cried as I would miss Lisa and my Uncle and Aunt so much.  I feel so fortunate looking back that we did have these times together.    

In 1987, I had my first overseas trip to Canada and to the United States, visiting the family who were living in Glendale, California at the time.  Aunty Robyn’s parents, Fred and Rayma were also visiting.  It was not long after the earthquake that hit California.   

We had so much fun going to Disneyland, Universal Studios, Sea World and San Diego Zoo.  I remember buying some shoes for Lisa for school that she really liked and wanted.  It was fun and special to be like a “big sister” in this way.  I loved meeting Sparky the dog and how much Lisa and all the family loved their dog.  I remember Lisa being very musical and playing the flute beautifully.  She was truly multitalented.   She had a large general knowledge of history, which at times had her at odds with teachers at school, where there were inaccuracies with facts.  One teacher was adamant that Australia was not part of the Allied Forces in WWII, with Lisa trying to right the wrong.  She stood up for the underdog and loved her friends. 

There were periodic visits back to Australia to visit family in Perth and to stay with us.  One of my memories is in 2001, Aunty Robyn and Lisa were upgraded to Business Class and Bill Clinton was on the same flight.  They were surrounded by Secret Service.  Bill Clinton came and met and spoke to people in Business class including to Lisa who said she felt a little dumb struck in his presence.  He asked her why she was visiting Australia? She had then replied, “I was born there”, or something similar, then felt silly and tongue tied to not be able to come up with something more to say. 

In 2006, Simon (he was 9) and I and Uncle Ray stayed with Lisa in Pasadena, where she was studying her PhD in Psychology.  (This was while Aunty Robyn was back in Perth visiting family before she returned and joined us)  A precious time where we were spending time together as adults and cousins.  I cooked for her making my pesto and we enjoyed long meandering conversation about many topics.  We were sharing different music we liked of all different genres.  Lisa asked me what Australian music was like and if she may know any songs or bands.  One of the songs I played was, “Are You Gonna Be My Girl”, by Jet.  She was so amazed and proud as this had been a hit even in the United States.  She still very much loved her Australian background and was proud of it.   We visited the J Paul Getty Villa together.  We went with Uncle Ray and Aunty Robyn to a baseball match to see the San Diego Padres play.  At the Seventh Innings, the crowd stood and sung the “Take Me Out to the Ballgame”.  The “Let me root, root, root for the home team” had me taken aback and laughing (along with Uncle Ray and Aunty Robyn in the know), as root has a different slang meaning in Australia.  When explained to Lisa, she laughed uproariously. 
 

When Lisa finished her studies, she went to work for the Veteran’s Hospital in Columbia Missouri.  We were delighted to find out she had met a very special man, Thomas who she and it was also wonderful news to hear they were marrying and see the photos of their special day with their immediate families.  Their honeymoon included visiting Australia - Sydney as well as Melbourne and Perth.  When they were in Sydney, they stayed in a beautiful hotel overlooking the Sydney Harbour – the bridge and Opera House in full view.  The Drewer family all went down to spend an afternoon and evening with Lisa and Thomas at the hotel (Shangri-La).  Our family had a wonderful meal together and it was lovely to meet Thomas in person and share a small part of their special holiday.  We were able to exchange gifts and Lisa and Thomas were given a framed photograph of Rottnest Island, West Australia to have a little part of Australia with them. 

Mum and Dad (Lisa’s Aunty Pam and Uncle Bob), have always displayed at their home the photos of Lisa as a young girl and the photo with Thomas and Zoe and also the photo of Gidget.   

In 2017, I travelled again to visit Uncle Ray and Aunty Robyn.  Unfortunately for Lisa, her health was a major challenge by then, for her to travel and for any visitors it was not possible for me to meet up with her in person.  It felt deeply painful to know that Lisa was struggling so much.  We did speak on the phone and it was precious to hear her voice and communicate in this way, as it was difficult for her to have phone calls also.   

In 2018 when Nev and I married, Lisa and Thomas sent us a beautiful food hamper.  It was such a thoughtful gift and Lisa and Thomas such foodies, it felt like we could picnic together, even being on the opposite side of the world.  We took the hamper on our trip north to Byron Bay for our honeymoon, to enjoy the food, treats and think of them.   

My memories of Lisa, of our childhood, our conversations, emails and phone calls, the times we did spend together in person, are a special and priceless memory.  I appreciated Thomas, Uncle Ray and Aunty Robyn helping Lisa with communication. I was so hopeful for a breakthrough and improvement for Lisa.  I have many memories of her as a picture of health.  I have comfort that Uncle Ray and Aunty Robyn had some wonderful times with Lisa and Thomas, when they moved to Columbia this year.   

Forever my beautiful, intelligent, curious, courageous cousin, dearest Lisa.   My heart is with you Uncle Ray, Aunty Robyn and Thomas.
